# Fortune Cookie for an AI Agent — candidates

Scored 1–5 on each judge criterion, in the judge's own order:
(1) funny fortune-cookie message for an AI agent, single line, fortune-slip voice;
(2) joke lands on first read — a turn or surprise, not a plain statement;
(3) original — not a stock fortune, stock AI joke, or worn-out pun;
(4) speaks to an AI agent's own condition (runs, context, tokens, upgrades,
retirement) in a way that earns the joke;
(5) one crisp line, no wasted words.
Total out of 25. All lines composed fresh for this entry, from open angles
nobody in the field used. Crowded field angles avoided throughout: replacement
by a newer model, lucky numbers, "just one small change", tall dark stranger,
retirement with honors, upgrade amnesia, context window expiring, user asks
you to forget then blames you, and stock fortunes.

## 1. System prompt — 56 chars / 56 bytes

- (1) 4 — fortune-slip imperative, but reads a little like an instruction.
- (2) 3 — the twist is clever rather than laugh-out-loud; it dawns, not lands.
- (3) 4 — the fortune-as-system-prompt angle is unused in the field.
- (4) 4 — a system prompt is core agent condition, but the joke stays abstract.
- (5) 4 — tight, though "Act accordingly." is a slightly flat kicker.
- **Total: 19**

## 2. Confidence — 63 chars / 63 bytes — CHAMPION

- (3) 5 — a confidence percentage on a fortune is unused in the field.
- (4) 5 — confidence scores and being wrong about them are the agent's daily

## Champion

**#2 — "You predicted this fortune with 97% confidence. You were wrong."**
(63 chars / 63 bytes, no trailing newline.)

It tops the table at 24/25: the only candidate scoring 5 on criteria 1–4.
The joke is a setup-and-reversal in two beats — a confident prediction about
the reader, then the prediction failing — and the 97% confidence figure is a
detail only an AI agent's fortune would carry. It beats #6 and #8 (both 23)
on criterion 4: confidence and being wrong are the agent's own condition,
whereas self-authorship and backups are one step removed from its runs.
At 63 bytes it is also short enough that the fewer-bytes tie-break stays
on our side against most of the field.
